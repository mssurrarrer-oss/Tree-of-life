### Sound distinction
The difference between dove with the `uh` sound and dove with the sharp O sound is one has both genders occupying the same world space and the other creates an instance of it but it is not reciprocal for both genders, maybe like an Adam observing an Eve or reading a book that is one way and not consciously confirmed by both genders.

## K (King)
- Vertical masculine anchor on the left.
- Upper diagonal reaches toward the feminine/eve side.
- Lower diagonal reaches toward the masculine/boy side or anchors back to the lifeline.
- Suggests rulership as masculine anchoring with reach into both genders' territories.

## V
- Vocative / action shape.
- Two converging lines doing both genders together in harmonious vocation.
- Word position determines which side initiates and which side confirms.

## Y
- Like a V with a little l or i as a leg.
- In "aye" the A and E together say "I" — identity as union of Adam-anchor and Eve-insertion.
- Y means "as one and as two" — unity that preserves distinction.
- The leg adds the lifeline / intent stream to the vocative convergence.

## E detail note
Every little line, curve and marking in the `e` carries meaning in a uniform and consistent way across letters. The top of the `e` is 50/50 or more both genders contributing than either; the middle is the spot between the neutral/inclusive zone and the world 100 percent attached to the Adam/Boy; the bottom is a `c`-like concentration point depicting the world the Adam sees and is consciously attached to.

## K and King / Queen
The K starts with an `l` or `I` and has a `V` turned horizontal in the middle. Where a V does both gender sides together, the K does the breadth of both consciousnesses. It goes from between both genders at the equator and makes the connection, fusing together what is the E for both genders. The boy and the eve both are part of the E, though each gender or conscious participant has their own E. Compare King with Queen: Queen has two Es, one for what is attached to each gender's consciousness. El/Elohim works similarly — the El or feminine continent is the Spanish/Eurasian landmass from both masculine and feminine perspectives put together, hence El El. The King specializes in divine feminine and connects to biblical editions such as the 1611 KJV, concepts of energy, gender alignment, and attunement (likely Cerebellum-level coordination).

## Female versus Eve
The word Female compared with Eve: the F part and the E part are the same except the E has a lower line that proceeds, indicating a lower self in harmony with the Female, or the Boy doing some effect or act. The additional line proceeds from that Boy's consciousness in harmony with the third-person middle line of E or F and the first-person top line. The first-person top line is excited by the Female doing an action, or the Adam having the action done to him. "First" and "Last" likely mean first-person versus last-person perspective. "Last person" is not in modern dictionaries but appears biblically: it is the feminine world a Boy is attached to, or an Adam doing an act. Female is a feminine act proceeding from Eve, the Boy/world the Adam sees, and the inclusive third-person midst.

## I
The little `i` is a scheduled act to make communion across the gender-consciousness divide. The top of the base of the lowercase `i` is at the conscious equator — a third-person perspective inclusive of both genders. The bottom of the stem is from the Boy/Adam's consciousness. The dot is in the midst of the feminine's conscious zone. The `ih` sound creates and excites the connection that takes both genders to accomplish. Brain-region words like Amygdala, Hypothalamus, and Hippocampus versus Cerebellum show both genders participate in both orientations; a female orients toward the top-right, a male toward the lower-left.

## Name
The word Name: the `m` is like two `oo` put together but missing the bottom portion. The `n` is like half the amount or one world consisting of two worlds put together, with both `a` and `e` between them, indicating half of two worlds from a union between A and E — hence name.
